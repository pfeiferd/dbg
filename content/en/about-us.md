## The German Borreliosis Society

The Deutsche Borreliose-Gesellschaft e.V. brings together scientists and physicians who deal with borreliosis and associated infectious diseases. Its aim is to develop and promote scientific and practical knowledge about the complex and varied clinical picture of Lyme borreliosis, particularly at an advanced stage.

In detail, the society pursues the following aims:

- holding congresses and workshops to promote
  - professional exchange between research, hospitals and medical practice, and
  - continuing medical education in line with the guidelines of the Medical Association,
- promoting science and research in order to advance the understanding, diagnosis and treatment of this widespread disease,
- providing consultative advice to other physicians in difficult cases of borreliosis, and
- drawing up guidelines on the diagnosis and treatment of Lyme borreliosis.

## Membership

The Deutsche Borreliose-Gesellschaft e.V. welcomes new members who take an interest in the work of the DBG and in exchanging views on Lyme borreliosis. In doing so you help to stimulate research into this disease and support the society's work in education and training around it. Members exchange their experience with one another, and once a year a conference takes place, with interesting talks from research and practice followed by an opportunity for personal exchange.

The members of the DBG are physicians, or scientists and laboratory diagnosticians working in the field of tick-borne and associated diseases. In addition, anyone may support the DBG as a sustaining member.

**Benefits of membership**

- exchange of experience with colleagues
- invitations to the events of the German Borreliosis Society
- reduced conference fees
- the opportunity to help shape the society

Donations and membership fees are tax-deductible (in Germany).

To become a member of the Deutsche Borreliose-Gesellschaft e.V., please download the membership application, the sustaining-member declaration or the declaration of support as a PDF file, fill it in and send it signed by post, fax or email (as a scan) to the address given in the form. Thank you very much!

## History

<img src="gasthof-schwaenlein.jpg" alt="Gasthof Schwänlein" title="Gasthof Schwänlein" style="max-width:420px">

In November 2003 the members of an association for the promotion of a therapy centre decided to wind it up. At the inn "Zum Schwänlein" in Nuremberg the idea arose of founding, instead, a medical society devoted more broadly to Lyme borreliosis. This led to the founding of the Deutsche Borreliose-Gesellschaft e.V. in 2004. The following passages about its early days are quoted from a speech given on its tenth anniversary:

*Unlike, presumably, most medical and scientific societies, it was not a shared professional and technical interest among colleagues that led to its foundation, but personal affliction, gathered around the ideas and analyses of the pathologist Prof. Johannes Roßner.*

*Unlike other societies, the DBG was not founded out of an institution, a university or a hospital; it really did start at the very bottom — and considering the circumstances and starting conditions of a number of its founding members, not on the ground floor but in the basement, two or three levels below.*

All the more ambitious were the aims of the founders, and had the chair implemented every good suggestion made by the members, the DBG would by now be on the upper floor of a high-rise.

The motivated founders wanted a medical society that promotes science and bridges the gap between science and practical application.

- That is why the society was dedicated to scientific discourse and to holding events such as today's.
- That is why the statutes require a scientific advisory board.
- That is why an honorary membership may be awarded for outstanding research, and
- that is why it was decided in 2007 that only physicians may be admitted as full members with voting rights.

**What was achieved in the first ten years?**

The number of members rose quickly and today stands at around 265.

In 2007 Willy Burgdorfer, who is closely connected with the DBG personally, was honoured with an honorary membership.

In 2009 he was able to attend the annual conference in person, at which Uwe Neubert and Vera Preac-Mursic were also appointed honorary members.

In 2008 the DBG was able to issue recommendations on the diagnosis and treatment of Lyme borreliosis for the first time.

In 2010 these recommendations were thoroughly revised and extended.

In 2011 the DBG took part in the further development of the S1 guidelines on cutaneous Lyme borreliosis and neuroborreliosis.

Conferences with international participation are held regularly. In 2012 this resulted in a special scientific issue, and last year, under the direction of Prof. Reiber, in an intensive working discussion with the manufacturers of cerebrospinal fluid tests.
